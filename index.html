Sichuan Food

Numbing, spicy, fresh, fragrant - this is the taste of the street.

Dandan noodles

Springy noodles, fragrant minced pork; a drizzle of chili oil, ground Sichuan pepper and scallion - and an old Chengdu street wakes up.

Bobo chicken

Skewers steeped in green-peppercorn or chili-oil broth, numbing and fragrant - best on a summer day with a bowl of cold liangxia.

Ice jelly

Hand-rubbed ice jelly, silky smooth, with brown-sugar syrup, hawthorn flakes and peanuts - the champion of cooling down the chili.

Twice-cooked pork

Pork belly curls into little lamp-bowl shapes, tossed with green garlic shoots and Pixian broad-bean paste - the undisputed king of home-style Sichuan cooking.

More Sichuan flavors

Mapo Tofu

Numbing, spicy, fragrant, tender and piping hot - the first dish of Sichuan cuisine; one spoonful, two bowls of rice.

Fuqi Feipian

Beef offal in chili oil: numbing, spicy, sweet aftertaste - the king of cold dishes.

Zhong Dumplings

Thin skins, tender filling, sweet-spicy red oil - an old Chengdu brand.

Long Wontons

Red-oil wontons with thin wrappers and a rich broth - a Chengdu snack classic.

Sichuan dialect snippets

Don't understand? Learn two phrases and you will blend right in.

"巴适得板" bā shì dé bǎn

Perfectly comfortable, extremely good. Any Chengdu local says this after one bite of proper dandan noodles.

"要得" yào dé

OK / sure / fine. The all-purpose yes - especially handy when someone invites you to hotpot.

"摆龙门阵" bǎi lóng mén zhèn

To chat, to shoot the breeze. Pick up a lidded tea in a teahouse and you can chat the whole afternoon away.

"不存在" bú cún zài

No problem / you're welcome. Chengdu-style straightforwardness - people say it even after doing you a favour.

"雄起" xióng qǐ

Come on / hang in there. A stadium chant, and the never-give-in spirit of Sichuan people.

"瓜娃子" guā wá zi

Silly goose. Mostly playful banter between friends, never meant harshly.

"抓子" zhuā zi

What are you doing / what's up? "你在抓子？" = "What are you up to?" - the all-purpose Chengdu street greeting.

"抵拢倒拐" dǐ lǒng dǎo guǎi

Walk to the end, then turn. The classic Chengdu way to give directions - far more local than "50 metres, then right".

"惊风火扯" jīng fēng huǒ chě

To make a big fuss, to panic. Describes someone who turns the smallest thing into a scene.

"打梦脚" dǎ mèng jiǎo

To zone out. Picture the boss catching you on your phone in a meeting: "莫打梦脚了哈！"

"搓甲甲" cuō jiá jiá

To scrub yourself in the bath, or the dirt you scrub off. A hot-spring and bathhouse word - pure street talk.

"抿抿甜" mǐn mǐn tián

Very sweet. Sichuan loves reduplication - 冰粉 抿抿甜, peaches 抿抿甜 - it sounds sweet just saying it.

T

Sichuan trivia

The Sichuan you don't know is even more addictive than hotpot

🍵

10,000 Teahouses

Chengdu has over 10,000 teahouses, one of the densest teahouse cities in the world. One lidded tea, one long chat, and the afternoon is gone.

🍲

20,000 Hotpot Restaurants

Chengdu has around 20,000 hotpot restaurants and 150,000 eateries. Eating is engraved in this city's daily life.

💴

The World's First Paper Money

In the Northern Song dynasty, Chengdu merchants invented the world's earliest paper money, "jiaozi" - more than 600 years before Europe.

☕

No.2 in China for Cafés

Chengdu has over 5,000 independent coffee shops, second in China only to Shanghai. Old teahouses and new cafés do not conflict here.

🍵

The Three-Piece Lidded Teacup

Bowl, lid and saucer stand for heaven covering it, earth carrying it and people nurturing it. Scrape the lid once and you have the Chengdu rhythm.

🗣️

Where "Longmen Zhen" Comes From

It comes from the storytelling classic "Xue Rengui Conquers the East" - his battle formation was so unpredictable that "setting up a Longmen formation" became a byword for chatting.

Travel Routes

Suggested multi-day itineraries for overseas visitors

🐼

One Day in Chengdu

1 day

A city sampler for a short stop: pandas in the morning, old streets after lunch, tea and hotpot to close the day.

  1. 1.Giant Panda Base - arrive for the 8:30 opening, the cubs are liveliest early
  2. 2.Wuhou Shrine & Jinli - a Three Kingdoms shrine, then snacks and face-changing
  3. 3.People's Park - a bowl of lidded tea and an ear cleaning at Heming
  4. 4.Sichuan hotpot - beef-tallow broth, tripe and duck intestine to finish

Best for: a short city stop

⛰️

Aba 3-Day Loop

3 days

West Sichuan in one loop: an ancient irrigation system, turquoise lakes and travertine pools.

  1. 1.Dujiangyan & Qingcheng Mountain - a 2,000-year-old weir and the Taoist mountain
  2. 2.Jiuzhaigou - turquoise lakes and tiered waterfalls, stay a night at the gate
  3. 3.Huanglong - terraced travertine pools, cable car up and walk down
  4. 4.Back to Chengdu - the Min River valley road, with stops for photos

Best for: nature and mountain lovers

🙏

Leshan & Mount Emei

2 days

Two days of Buddhist Sichuan: the giant stone Buddha, then a sunrise above the sea of clouds.

  1. 1.Leshan Giant Buddha - the 71 m stone Maitreya, walk the Nine-Bend Plank Road
  2. 2.Mount Emei - ride up, sleep at the Golden Summit, wake for the sunrise
  3. 3.Baoguo Temple - the temple at the foot of the mountain before you leave

Best for: Buddhist culture and heritage

H

Bashu Intangible Cultural Heritage

Traditional arts passed down for generations in Sichuan

🎭

Sichuan Opera Face-Changing

Performers swap painted masks in the blink of an eye - a flick of the sleeve, a turn of the head, and a new face appears. The trick is a stage secret handed down only to apprentices.

Where to see it: tea houses and the opera stage at Jinli, Chengdu

🪡

Shu Embroidery

One of China's four great embroidery schools, made in Chengdu for over 2,000 years. Silk threads are split finer than a hair, so a single panel of flowers, fish or pandas can take months.

Where to see it: the Shu Embroidery Museum and studio workshops in Chengdu

🎋

Sichuan Bamboo Weaving

Craftspeople split bamboo into strips as thin as paper and weave them into baskets, lanterns and even teapots. The shapes hold without a single nail or a drop of glue.

Where to see it: Daoming Bamboo Town, about an hour from Chengdu

🍬

Sugar Figurine Blowing

Hot sugar is pulled, pinched and blown through a straw into little animals that glow in the light. Half candy, half sculpture, the craft is more than 600 years old.

Where to see it: temple fairs and street corners in the old towns

🏮

Chengdu Shadow Puppetry

Flat leather puppets are pressed against a lit cloth screen and moved on sticks, so their shadows act out old stories. One performer sings, beats the drum and works the figures at the same time.

Where to see it: lantern festivals and the shadow-puppet troupes of Chengdu

🍲

Sichuan Hotpot Craft

The base is beef tallow simmered for hours with Pixian broad-bean paste, chillies and Sichuan pepper before it reaches the table. Cooking times are a local language of their own: tripe goes seven up, eight down.

Where to see it: the old hotpot houses all over Chengdu
